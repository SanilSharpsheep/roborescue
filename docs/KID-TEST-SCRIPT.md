# RoboRescue: kid test script (grades 3–5)

Goal: find where kids get confused. You are testing the game, not the kid.

## Setup (5 min)
- 5 kids, grades 3–5, one at a time, about 15 minutes each. Use the device they will really use (iPad, Chromebook, or phone).
- Open the live game fresh (clear saved data so the first-time guide shows).
- Ask a parent or teacher for permission first. Do not record faces or names. Take notes only.
- Sit beside the kid, not in front of the screen.

## Say this first
"I made a game and I want to see if it makes sense. If something is confusing, that is my mistake, not yours. Please say what you are thinking out loud. I won't help unless you ask."

## Tasks (do not explain how)
1. Start the game and make your rescuer.
2. Say in your own words what this game is about.
3. Build a bot and test it on Mission 1.
4. If the bot gets stuck: find out why and fix it.
5. Turn the music off.
6. Find your badge.
7. (Optional) Do the quiz. Use the "read it to me" button.

## What to write down (a tally is fine)
| Moment | Notes |
|---|---|
| Hesitated more than 5 seconds (where?) | |
| Tapped the wrong thing (what?) | |
| Asked "what do I do?" | |
| Could not find a button (which?) | |
| Said the bot and the rescuer are the same thing | |
| Words they did not understand | |
| Laughed or smiled (where?) | |
| Wanted to stop or looked bored (where?) | |

## Questions at the end (keep it short)
- What was your favorite part?
- What was confusing or hard?
- Who is the green gecko? Who is the silver robot? Who are you?
- Would you play it again? Why or why not?

## Success looks like
- 4 of 5 kids finish Mission 1 without help.
- 4 of 5 kids can say the game is about building robots to help after a flood.
- No kid confuses the rescuer with the Rescue Bot.
- Nobody cannot find the mute button.

## After the tests
Group the notes. Fix the problems that 2 or more kids hit first. Retest with 2 new kids.
